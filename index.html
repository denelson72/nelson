The Case

What This Is About

Debra Nelson, a former employee of Curtiss-Wright — a major U.S. defense contractor involved in shipbuilding — filed a whistleblower complaint in 2023 after experiencing unlawful discrimination. What followed was a two-year coordinated campaign of retaliation involving cyber intrusion, physical surveillance, evidence destruction, and the failure of every federal institution designed to protect whistleblowers.

This site exists because the courts, the FBI, the Department of Defense Inspector General, the NLRB, the EEOC, and a sitting Congressman's office have all failed to act. The public record presented here is the last recourse. Sunlight is the best disinfectant.

The Central Unanswered Question

"How did Congressman Clyburn's office transmit a document referencing Ms. Nelson to the Department of Defense — before she had given that document to Congressman Clyburn's office?"

Judicial Misconduct

What Happened Inside the Courtroom

The misconduct in this case is not limited to events outside the courtroom. The judicial record itself documents a pattern of rulings that systematically blocked Ms. Nelson's ability to obtain evidence — and a direct contradiction between two judges regarding a court-issued subpoena.

The Central Judicial Misconduct Question

"Magistrate Judge Cherry reviewed the evidence and issued the subpoenas. Magistrate Judge Baker — her replacement — then withdrew those same subpoenas and told Ms. Nelson the court had never issued them. The prior signed order remains on the docket."

January 21, 2025
Case Removed to Federal Court — Counsel Immediately Withdraws
Defendants remove the case from state court to US District Court. Shortly thereafter, Plaintiff's counsel Bonnie Hunt of Hunt Law LLC withdraws. Ms. Nelson has alleged that defense counsel and/or Curtiss-Wright compensated her former attorney to withdraw, leaving her to proceed pro se against a major defense contractor with full corporate legal resources.
Dkt. Nos. 10, 11, 12, 13, 19
2025
Magistrate Judge Cherry Reviews Evidence — Issues Subpoenas
The first presiding Magistrate Judge reviews Ms. Nelson's evidence and issues subpoenas authorizing discovery. This judicial approval validates Ms. Nelson's position that the evidence warranted further investigation. The signed subpoenas are on the court docket.
Court Docket — Signed Order
2025
Judge Baker Replaces Judge Cherry — Withdraws Court-Issued Subpoenas
Magistrate Judge Mary Gordon Baker assumes the case. She withdraws the subpoenas previously signed by Judge Cherry. When Ms. Nelson objects, Judge Baker tells her she is mistaken — that the court never issued the subpoenas. The prior signed order from Judge Cherry remains on the docket contradicting this claim directly.
Documented Contradiction — Court Record
September 16, 2025
Motion to Compel — Defendants Refuse to Produce Timekeeping Evidence
Ms. Nelson files a Motion to Compel requesting the timekeeping audit trail showing who altered her hours, when, and why. This evidence goes to the core of her discrimination claims. Defendants claim the records do not exist. Ms. Nelson believes this is untrue and that the records have been concealed.
Dkt. No. 105
October 8–9, 2025
Subpoena of Former Attorney — Objection Immediately Filed
Ms. Nelson files Notices of Service of Subpoena seeking financial records and communications between Curtiss-Wright, defense counsel, and her former attorney Bonnie Hunt — to prove bias and collusion following her sudden withdrawal. On October 9, attorney Andrew Countryman immediately files an objection on behalf of Hunt Law LLC.
Dkt. Nos. 164, 171, 172
October 20, 2025
Judge Baker Denies All Motions — Blocks Evidence of Attorney Collusion
In a 7-page order (Dkt. No. 192), Judge Baker denies Ms. Nelson's Motion to Compel and Motion to Strike in full. The court rules that communications between Bonnie Hunt and Curtiss-Wright have "absolutely no bearing" on the case — without allowing Ms. Nelson to obtain those records to prove otherwise. The court also denies inspection of Curtiss-Wright's premises, shielding the defendant from physical evidence discovery.
Dkt. No. 192 — Full Order Below
October 21, 2025
Judge Baker Grants Apple Protective Order — No Evidentiary Hearing, No Good Cause Shown
Without conducting an evidentiary hearing or requiring Apple to make a specific showing of "good cause" under Rule 26(c), Judge Baker grants Apple's Motion for Protective Order and prohibits Ms. Nelson from issuing any additional discovery to Apple. Apple submitted no affidavit or specific evidence of undue burden beyond general inconvenience. The order describes Ms. Nelson's motions as "premature and/or frivolous" — a characterization Ms. Nelson disputes given that the September 10, 2025 incident post-dated the subpoena and involved directly relevant device logs Apple never produced.
ECF No. 194 — Protective Order Order
November 6, 2025
Ms. Nelson Files Rule 72(a) Objections — Both Orders Clearly Erroneous
Ms. Nelson files formal objections to both Judge Baker orders under Fed. R. Civ. P. 72(a), addressed to District Judge Richard M. Gergel. On the Hunt Law order: the subpoena was reviewed and issued by Judge Cherry's office after a nine-day judicial review period — the Clerk cannot issue a Rule 45 subpoena for a pro se party without judicial review, making Judge Baker's finding that "this Court did not expressly approve the subpoena" demonstrably false. On the Apple order: Rule 26(c)(1) requires "good cause" and Apple showed none. The blanket prohibition on all future Apple discovery exceeds what Rule 45(d)(3)(A) permits.
Fed. R. Civ. P. 72(a) Objections — Filed
December 1, 2025
Judge Gergel Promptly Grants Apple's Extension — Ms. Nelson's Motions Remain Unaddressed
On December 1, 2025, Judge Gergel promptly grants Apple's Motion for Extension of Time (ECF No. 226) to file a reply to Ms. Nelson's objections. Meanwhile, Ms. Nelson's own pending motions — including her November 24 request to depose additional witnesses — remain unruled upon. The disparity in judicial responsiveness to Apple versus the pro se plaintiff is documented on the docket.
ECF No. 232 — Text Order — Exhibit F
December 8–9, 2025
Fogarty Sends Letter Directly to the Court — Contents Hidden from Ms. Nelson

Discovery Obstruction

How Evidence Is Being Blocked

Beyond the courtroom rulings, Ms. Nelson has documented a pattern of witness interference, subpoena manipulation, and procedural irregularities that have systematically prevented her from obtaining evidence — despite diligent efforts across multiple federal districts.

🚫

Witness Brian Fogarty — Coached to Defy Subpoena

Fogarty was disciplined for bullying Ms. Nelson during her employment and is a key comparator witness. After missing his deposition and receiving a Rule 45 notice from Ms. Nelson, he sent a written "cease and desist" letter stating she "does not have the power of the court" — a claim he could only have received from defense counsel. The letter expressly states he was "informed by the defendant's legal counsel" about deposition limits.

Exhibit A — Fogarty Letter (Dec 1, 2025)
🚫

Witness Jeff Womack — Two Missed Depositions, Zero Accountability

Fact witness Jeff Womack missed two scheduled depositions. He provided no medical documentation, filed no written objection, and sought no relief from the Court. Defense counsel has never produced Womack or proposed an alternate date. As of the December 8, 2025 Supplement, no ruling on Womack's noncompliance has been issued by the Court.

Ongoing — No Court Order Issued
📄

Google Subpoena Withdrawn Without Notice

Ms. Nelson obtained a properly signed, court-issued subpoena to Google in person at the Northern District of California on October 31, 2025. It was withdrawn and invalidated on November 6 — with no email, mailing, or docket notification to Ms. Nelson. She learned of this only on December 2 when she called the Clerk's Office. She had already filed a Motion to Compel on November 21 — not knowing the subpoena had been pulled.

Exhibits G & H — Confirmed by Clerk Email
🍎

Apple Device Logs — Permanently Blocked

Ms. Nelson's subpoena to Apple sought device-level logs, remote access records, and PIN unlock attempts relevant to her cyber intrusion claims. Apple's production stopped before a September 10, 2025 incident Ms. Nelson reported directly to Apple. Judge Baker then issued a blanket protective order prohibiting all future discovery to Apple — without an evidentiary hearing or specific showing of harm from Apple under Rule 26(c)(1).

ECF No. 194 — Rule 72(a) Objection Pending
📬

Fogarty's Letter to the Court — Hidden from Ms. Nelson

Brian Fogarty sent a letter directly to the US District Court concerning Ms. Nelson's subpoenas and conduct. It appears on the docket only as a generic "correspondence" entry — with no contents available to Ms. Nelson. The pro se plaintiff cannot see or respond to what a subpoenaed witness submitted to the Court about her own case. Ms. Nelson's December 9, 2025 letter to the Clerk requests a copy and written policy clarification.

ECF No. 249 — Access Denied
⚖️

Hunt Law Subpoena — Court Contradicts Its Own Docket

Judge Cherry's case manager confirmed the Hunt Law subpoena was held nine days for judicial review before issuance. Judge Baker later ruled "this Court did not expressly approve the subpoena" — a statement directly contradicted by the docket, the timeline, and the Clerk's own issuance process. Judge Baker's order constitutes a de facto reversal of a prior judicial act without notice or findings. Ms. Nelson's Rule 72(a) objection to this ruling is pending before Judge Gergel.

ECF No. 192 — Rule 72(a) Objection Pending

"Every avenue for obtaining electronic evidence has been closed — Apple is protected by court order, Google's subpoena was quietly withdrawn, and witnesses are being coached by defense counsel to claim Ms. Nelson lacks judicial authority. These are not coincidences."

Public Petition

Demand Congressional Action

We, the undersigned, call upon the United States House of Representatives — and specifically the House Judiciary Committee — to immediately resume oversight hearings and open a formal investigation into the following matters arising from the case of Debra Nelson v. Curtiss-Wright (Case No. 2:25-CV-00405-RMG-MGB, US District Court, District of South Carolina):

(1) Alleged judicial misconduct in an active federal lawsuit; (2) The destruction of evidence — a cell phone taken into police custody that was explicitly identified on video as evidence in a federal lawsuit — 100 days after being taken into custody; (3) The transmission of Ms. Nelson's private documents by Congressman Clyburn's office to the Department of Defense before Ms. Nelson had authorized or provided those documents; (4) The failure of the FBI to investigate three formal reports of cyber intrusion and physical retaliation filed by Ms. Nelson; (5) The coordinated closure of whistleblower complaints across multiple federal agencies — the DOD IG, NLRB, and EEOC — using each other's closures as justification; (6) Ongoing discrimination at Curtiss-Wright as revealed through deposition testimony.

The House Judiciary Committee has both the authority and the obligation to investigate these matters. We demand that oversight committee meetings be unpaused and that this case receive a full, public, and independent congressional review.
